Financial Ratios

Formulas, what they measure, and what a high or low result tells you.

Building Blocks

Gross Profit
Total Revenue − Cost of Goods Sold
Gross Sales
All sales, before any deductions
Net Sales (Revenue)
Gross Sales − (Allowances + Discounts + Returns)
Average (Balance)
(Starting + Ending) ÷ 2

Profitability

Gross Profit Ratio

Gross ProfitNet Sales
× 100
HighStrong markup / pricing power over what it costs to make or buy the goods sold. Good cost control on production or purchasing.
LowThin margin between selling price and cost of goods — heavy discounting, rising input costs, or weak pricing control.

Net Profit Ratio

Net ProfitTotal Revenue
× 100
HighBusiness keeps a large share of revenue as profit after ALL expenses (operating, interest, tax) — efficient overall cost control.
LowOperating expenses, interest or overheads are eating into profit even if gross profit looks fine — a sign to check expense lines below gross profit.

Compare Gross Profit Ratio and Net Profit Ratio together: a high gross margin but low net margin points to bloated operating expenses, not a pricing problem.

Return on Owners Equity

Net ProfitAverage Equity Balance
× 100
HighOwners are earning a strong return on the capital they've invested — efficient use of equity, attractive to investors.
LowOwners' funds are tied up but not generating much return — capital could arguably be working harder elsewhere.

Your source sheet says "Net Revenue" over Average Equity Balance — the standard formula uses Net Profit over Average Equity. Worth double-checking against your course notes before the exam.

Efficiency & Turnover

Rate of Turnover of Inventories

Cost of Goods SoldAverage Inventory
HighStock is sold and replaced quickly — strong sales or lean stock-holding. Extremely high can mean stock levels are too low, risking stockouts.
LowWeak demand, poor forecasting, or overstocking — cash is tied up sitting in inventory rather than being put to use.

Rate of Turnover of Accounts Receivable

Net Credit SalesAverage Accounts Receivable
HighDebtors are being converted to cash quickly — effective credit control and collection processes.
LowCustomers are slow to pay — cash flow is squeezed and there's greater risk of bad debts. May signal credit terms are too generous.

Turnover in Days

365Turnover Ratio
High (more days)Takes longer to sell inventory or collect debts — slower cycle, more cash tied up for longer.
Low (fewer days)Inventory sells or debtors pay faster — quicker cash cycle, generally the healthier sign.

Note this one flips direction versus the ratios above it: for turnover ratios, high is usually good; for turnover in days, low is usually good, since it's measuring time taken rather than speed of activity.
